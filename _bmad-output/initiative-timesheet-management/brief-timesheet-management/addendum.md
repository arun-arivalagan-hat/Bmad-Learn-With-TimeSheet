Detail that belongs behind the brief. Source: the spec and PRD of 2026-10-11.

## Retention

Records are kept for three years, a generic rule modelled on US practice. India's 5-year rule is not adopted, there was no legal review, and the owner accepts the risk.

## Sizing history

The first version is sized for 160 employees and 20 projects (owner confirmed). The PRFAQ figures of about 10,000 employees and about 1,000 projects are superseded.

## Pain figure

Project managers spend about 19 hours a week collecting hours. This is the owner's figure and has not been verified. It is the baseline for the success measure on project-manager time.

## Open decisions

- Whether a waived late mark counts as on time in the on-time measure.
- The grace period after an HR system outage.
- Whether the 40-hour cap applies to later corrections, and the time limit and employee dispute route for them.

## Working defaults (provisional, not final)

- Escalation wait for an unresolved review: 3 working days, then the business owner is contacted.
- "Unusual sheet" check: a weekly total differing from the prior week by more than 15 hours, or hours on a closed project.
- Employee reminder 6 hours before the deadline; reviewer reminder Monday 09:00 IST.
- HR system data counts as fresh for 5 minutes.
- Reviewers see their own project's block plus total hours.

## Spec and PRD inconsistency to fix

Both say the escalation wait and the sweep frequency are "not set", while their own provisional-defaults lists give 3 working days and a daily sweep. The spec log also lacks the owner's confirmation of CAP-6 and CAP-7 that the PRD log records.
