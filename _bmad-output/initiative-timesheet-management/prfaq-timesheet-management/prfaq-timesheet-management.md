# (press release draft in progress)

<!-- coaching-notes-stage-1 -->
**Concept type:** internal tool (an HRMS module); framing is stakeholder value and adoption, not pricing.

**Primary customer (owner decision):** the project manager. The project manager opens the weekly sheet (as the Project 1 owner) and approves or rejects the hours.

**Other people named as feeling the pain:** the employee's reporting manager (no role in the spec today) and the HR person who manages the Excel files. The employee fills the sheet in and is the person who must change behaviour.

**The pain, with the owner's numbers (not independently verified):**
- About 19 hours a week per project manager spent collecting and checking hours.
- About 10 people per project manager, one project each, across about 1,000 projects (about 10,000 employees if one manager per project; the owner has not confirmed the scale).
- If every project has its own manager, that is about 19,000 hours a week, roughly 475 full-time people. This is the coach's arithmetic and an assumption, to be confirmed.

**The worst consequence the owner named:** HR reduces an employee's hike percentage and score when timesheets are late or wrong. HR decides. This means the organisation uses timesheets in performance decisions today.

**Decision A (owner, 2026-10-10):** HR decides the consequences of late and wrong timesheets. The tool keeps the record accurate and fair and never withholds pay itself. This contradicts the spec's "hours are never a performance score" and the brief's matching sentence; both were synced on 2026-10-10 (spec CAP-17 and CAP-18, new HR constraints; brief updated).

**Fairness rules proposed with decision A (to confirm in the PRFAQ):** a sheet never published on time cannot be marked late; an employee is not penalised for a sheet the reviewer approved; employees can see their own late marks; an approved extension waives the mark.

**Open before the press release:** confirm the scale (10,000 employees?), what the 19 hours is made of, and the after-target (hours per project manager after launch).
