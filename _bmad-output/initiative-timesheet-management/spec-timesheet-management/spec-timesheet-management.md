# Timesheet Management

## Why

Employees report weekly work on the HAT timesheet form, but the hours are not tied to billing or utilization. This initiative captures actual work effort per project and links it to billing and utilization, so billing and resourcing decisions rest on real hours rather than plans. It should also be quick and pleasant enough that people submit on time without being chased.

## Capabilities

- **CAP-1**
  - **intent:** An employee selects the timesheet week they are reporting.
  - **success:** A sheet cannot be submitted without a week; the chosen week is stored with the sheet.
- **CAP-2**
  - **intent:** An employee logs effort on up to 5 projects, each with project, tasks/activities and hours; the reviewer of each project comes from its HRMS assignment and is shown, not entered; Project 1 is the employee's primary project as identified in the HRMS.
  - **success:** A sheet with Project 1 filled and Projects 2-5 optionally filled is accepted; a block missing its project, tasks/activities or hours, or a sixth block, is refused; the reviewer is display-only, and a project with no HRMS reviewer assignment is flagged for resolution and its blocks route down the alternate chain; a leave-only sheet (CAP-23) carries no project blocks; the sheet carries an assignment-pending flag (an attribute, not a state) with one of four reason codes: missing primary project, conflicting primary project, missing owner or multiple owners; HR is notified for all four and the case is listed, with who, when, why and the reason code recorded, in an assignment-resolution list, where a primary-project problem and an owner-mapping problem stay distinct; a missing or conflicting primary project waives the Project 1 requirement while the flag is open, no Project 1 is guessed and no block is treated as Project 1, the employee may submit otherwise valid blocks, and each block routes by its own project's HRMS reviewer or down the alternate chain; a missing or multiple owners code does not waive it, because Project 1 is known, and the sheet goes to HR for publication after the cut-off (CAP-16); for a conflicting primary project or multiple owners every candidate owner is excluded from deciding that sheet; the flag leaves when the HRMS verifiably shows exactly one primary project and exactly one owner for the employee, a draft sheet then follows the normal Project 1 rule, and recorded submissions, decisions and lateness judgements are kept and never rewritten; publication snapshots the employee's primary project and owner, the sheet keeps that Project 1 and owner for status view, submit and extension routing, and a later HRMS transfer does not refuse the submit; if the HRMS primary project or owner then changes, the affected pending submission, decision or extension is flagged for resolution in the assignment-resolution list as a post-publication assignment change (not a fifth reason code) and no recorded decision is rewritten; a submit that cannot verify the HRMS data it needs is an HRMS-caused refusal (CAP-10).
- **CAP-3**
  - **intent:** An employee records PTO hours for the week at 8 hours per day.
  - **success:** PTO of 0 or a multiple of 8 is accepted; any other value is refused; on a leave-only sheet (CAP-23) PTO is derived from approved HRMS leave and never entered.
- **CAP-4**
  - **intent:** An employee records key accomplishments and key learnings, each learning tagged Beginner, Intermediate or Advanced.
  - **success:** A sheet without accomplishments, or with a learning lacking one of the three levels, is refused.
- **CAP-5**
  - **intent:** An employee submits the completed sheet, and the system records the submitter's name and email automatically.
  - **success:** A submitted sheet shows the submitter's name and email without the employee typing them; every required field is enforced at submit; a submit that needs HRMS data that cannot be verified fails closed (HRMS outage constraint).
- **CAP-6**
  - **intent:** The reviewer named for each project approves or rejects the hours in their project's block; a rejection carries a comment and shows the employee what was rejected.
  - **success:** A reviewer sees only sheets with hours against their project and decides only their own block, and each decision applies to the exact block version the reviewer saw (a decision on a stale version is refused with a message to reload); a rejected block returns to the employee alone, with the comment, for the employee to fix and resubmit; a rejection without a comment is refused; the sheet is approved and locked only when every block is approved; the Project 1 owner can never approve or reject, and the reporting manager decides only as the first eligible decider in the alternate chain (CAP-21, CAP-22).
- **CAP-7**
  - **intent:** Approved hours roll up by project, employee and week into billable vs non-billable hours and utilization, and finance can export them per project with an audit trail.
  - **success:** For a given week, the roll-up for each project equals the sum of its approved block hours plus dated adjustment entries (CAP-24), each approved block counted once; unapproved hours are excluded; the export reproduces the roll-up as of its issue and lists who approved each line and when; an issued export is never rewritten, and a later export carries the dated adjustment history; a leave-only sheet (CAP-23) reports zero project hours.
- **CAP-8**
  - **intent:** An employee starts a new week's sheet by copying the previous week's sheet and editing it.
  - **success:** Copying prefills projects and tasks from the prior week with hours left editable, and each project's reviewer is taken from the HRMS at the time of copying; the copy is a new sheet, and the original is unchanged; if the HRMS cannot be verified the copy is refused with a message to retry and no reviewer is taken from stale data.
- **CAP-9**
  - **intent:** An employee reopens rejected blocks and blocks no reviewer has decided yet to correct them, with every change recorded.
  - **success:** A rejected block, or a block no reviewer has decided, can be reopened and resubmitted by the employee who submitted the sheet; approved blocks are never reopened, and a change to approved hours goes through an adjustment (CAP-24); each resubmission of a block creates a new block version; an approval and a reopen on the same block cannot both succeed, so the first valid one wins and the other is told; the history shows who changed what and when.
- **CAP-10**
  - **intent:** An employee submits the published weekly sheet within 24 hours of its publication, and one who misses that deadline can still submit late, with a late penalty, or request an extension with a reason.
  - **success:** A sheet first submitted at or before its publication time plus 24 hours counts as on time, with the deadline stored in UTC, shown in IST and never shortened when publication is late or the deadline falls after the week ends; a later first submission is accepted, marked late, and carries a late penalty (a recorded late mark counted per employee, visible as set out in the constraints); lateness is judged on the first submission only, so a rejected and resubmitted sheet gains no late mark, and the resubmission time is kept as history without erasing the first submission time; an extension request records its reason, has its own status (pending, approved or declined), and is decided by the HRMS-assigned reviewer of the employee's Project 1 when eligible (taken from the HRMS assignment, so no submitted block is needed), by the eligible alternate chain when that reviewer is the employee, ineligible or conflicted as that sheet's Project 1 owner or delegate, or when the HRMS has no primary-project or reviewer assignment for the employee (also flagged for resolution), with only the routing class fixed when the request is made (the Project 1 reviewer or the alternate chain) and the deciding person re-evaluated, so a pending request reroutes under the same events as a block and never sticks on an ineligible person, eligibility being re-checked at decision time, and through the HR exception queue (CAP-22) when nobody is eligible; HR assigns a decider but never decides; an approved extension records a waiver, and the original late mark stays in the audit history, shown as waived and never deleted; an HRMS-caused refusal at submit is not a submission and fails closed (HRMS outage constraint); only a refused submit on the same sheet that the HRMS caused is a qualifying attempt (HRMS checks are validated before the over-40 check, and refused copies and publishes do not qualify); when the first successful submission falls after the deadline and a qualifying attempt was recorded before that submission, even one recorded after the deadline, or an observed HRMS outage window overlaps the period between publication and the deadline, no late mark counts automatically: the mark is recorded in the history as held, the sheet is flagged outage-affected, the actual submission time is kept and never backdated, and an extension request is raised automatically with the recorded attempt or outage as its reason and decided under the extension rules above, the decider judging the evidence (approved: the mark is shown as waived; declined: the mark counts with the actual submission time); a failed attempt recorded after the deadline only triggers that review and never by itself proves that an outage prevented submission before the deadline, so the decider uses system-recorded evidence and observed outage information where available, and the full audit history is kept; an employee who could not sign in and has no recorded attempt uses a normal extension request; the module records observed HRMS outage windows (start, end, how observed); no grace period or deadline-extension policy is set; none of these blocks submission.
- **CAP-11**
  - **intent:** A reviewer sees sheets with anomalies first and approves their own blocks on clean sheets in bulk.
  - **success:** A sheet with hours on a closed project, or a total differing from the prior week by more than 15 hours, is flagged and listed first; selecting all unflagged sheets approves, in one action, the reviewer's own block on each, every block validated separately against the version reviewed and approved individually; a sheet becomes approved only when every block is approved.
- **CAP-12**
  - **intent:** An employee gives a one-tap smiley rating after submitting, as a measure of how the process feels.
  - **success:** The rating is stored with the sheet, is optional, and never blocks or delays submit.
- **CAP-13**
  - **intent:** An employee sees a private weekly insight card of their hours by project.
  - **success:** The card shows the employee's own hours per project for a week and is visible only to them; learnings stay private unless the employee shares them.
- **CAP-14**
  - **intent:** Employees get a reminder when their sheet is unsubmitted, and project reviewers get a reminder when sheets await approval.
  - **success:** An employee with no submitted sheet for the week receives a reminder before the deadline; a project reviewer with pending sheets receives one; employees on approved leave receive none; at the Friday 12:00 PM IST cut-off the owner, any delegate and HR each receive one reminder that sheets are still unpublished.
- **CAP-15** (retired: the payroll handoff is out of scope; the id is never reused)
- **CAP-16**
  - **intent:** The owner of an employee's Project 1 (the primary project, taken from the employee's HRMS project assignment, not from the sheet) publishes that employee's weekly sheet, usually on Friday, which makes it available to that employee to fill in; the owner may name a delegate for the current timesheet week to publish in their place, and HR can publish a sheet that is still unpublished after Friday 12:00 PM IST (06:30 UTC) of its timesheet week.
  - **success:** Publication is per employee per week; an employee cannot start a week's sheet until it is published, and is notified when it is; an employee with any of the four assignment-pending reason codes (CAP-2: missing or conflicting primary project, missing or multiple owners) is flagged for resolution and never given a guessed owner, so only HR can publish that sheet after the cut-off and the unresolved assignment never makes the employee late; publication snapshots the employee's primary project and owner (CAP-2); publishing is separate from deciding, so an employee who is their own sheet's owner or delegate may publish it and still never decides any part of it; a publish that needs HRMS data that cannot be verified fails closed with a message to retry and the attempt is recorded, and the deadline counts only from a successful publication; publication records who published and when, whether owner, delegate or HR; the first successful publication wins and a published sheet is never published again; before the cut-off HR cannot publish, and after it the owner or delegate still can while the sheet is unpublished; designating a delegate is independent of the Eligible Decider rule and grants no decision eligibility: the delegate must be an active employee not on approved leave when named and be explicitly designated by the owner, and the naming must be in effect when publishing; a naming is the owner plus one timesheet week, for the current week only, covers all of that owner's employees' sheets for that week and expires at the Monday 00:00 IST that ends the week, so past weeks are published by the owner or HR; each naming, change and expiry is audited and pending decisions are re-evaluated when one occurs; a delegate who published a sheet stays excluded from deciding that same sheet after the naming expires, and historical actions are unchanged; the submission deadline is publication plus 24 hours, so a delay in publishing never makes an employee late.
- **CAP-17**
  - **intent:** HR can read each employee's approved hours, late marks and approval status per week, and whether a published sheet has been submitted.
  - **success:** HR sees approved hours, late marks and approval status for any employee and week; from the cut-off HR sees a list of unpublished sheets, which excludes leave-only sheets (CAP-23); HR sees the assignment-resolution list with reason codes and post-publication assignment changes (CAP-2) and late marks held for an outage with the evidence recorded (CAP-10); for a published sheet HR sees the employee, projects, publication time, deadline and publisher, and sees it as awaiting submission before the deadline and not submitted after it, with no late mark for a sheet never submitted; HR can view adjustment history for audit but cannot edit a sheet, its hours, its late marks or an adjustment entry, and never approves or rejects hours; its write actions are limited to publishing (CAP-16), assigning an alternate decider from the exception queue for a block, an extension request or an adjustment-needed flag (CAP-22), and raising an adjustment-needed flag with a reason (CAP-24), and in the HR role it never decides or records any of them.
- **CAP-18**
  - **intent:** An employee can see their own late marks.
  - **success:** An employee sees every late mark recorded against them, with the week and the deadline it missed, and sees no other employee's marks.
- **CAP-19**
  - **intent:** A reporting manager can read their direct reports' approved hours, late marks and approval status per week.
  - **success:** A reporting manager sees these for their direct reports only, and cannot edit or waive anything; deciding is possible only as the first eligible decider in the alternate chain (CAP-21, CAP-22), for a self-review block, a block whose reviewer is ineligible or conflicted, the matching extension request, or an adjustment (CAP-24), and never for a sheet where they are the employee or that sheet's Project 1 owner or delegate.
- **CAP-20**
  - **intent:** The Project 1 owner can see which of their published sheets were submitted on time, submitted late or are not yet submitted.
  - **success:** The owner sees each published sheet's submission status; the view is read-only and offers no approve or reject action.
- **CAP-21**
  - **intent:** No one decides a block on their own sheet: when the reviewer named for a project block is the employee who submitted the sheet, an alternate approver decides that block instead.
  - **success:** An employee never sees an approve or reject action on a block of their own sheet; such a block goes down a tiered alternate chain: the employee's designated reporting manager when eligible, and holders of the delivery-manager HRMS role only when the reporting manager is ineligible (any eligible holder in that tier may decide and the first valid decision wins), eligibility being the Eligible Decider rule in the constraints; the same tiering applies to blocks, extension requests and adjustments; when no decider is eligible the block enters the HR exception queue and HR assigns one (CAP-22); the alternate approver decides only that block; the history records that it was a self-review case and who decided it.
- **CAP-22**
  - **intent:** A block or an extension request whose normal decider cannot act or is conflicted, and an approved block that needs an adjustment when no eligible recorder exists, is handled by an eligible alternate, so it does not stay pending.
  - **success:** A block whose named reviewer is inactive in the existing HRMS, on approved leave, or conflicted as that sheet's Project 1 owner or delegate (ineligible for that sheet only) goes to the same tiered chain of eligible deciders as CAP-21; eligibility is checked when the block is routed, at each new block version, at decision time, on HRMS active or leave change events and by a periodic fallback sweep, with approved leave judged at the instant of the check; a reviewer on approved leave is ineligible to decide during that leave, so an eligible alternate handles the decision, and the block is rerouted promptly when its reviewer becomes ineligible; the named reviewer, when eligible, and the eligible alternate of the current tier (CAP-21) may both act after routing, only one valid decision can be recorded for a block and the other actor is told, and a block is never automatically routed back to a returning reviewer; when no decider is eligible the block, the extension request or the adjustment-needed flag enters an HR exception queue and HR is notified; HR assigns an alternate who passes the Eligible Decider rule and holds no HR role, an attempt to assign an HR-role holder, including the assigning user, is refused and logged, an HR-role holder may decide only in a separate capacity as a reporting manager or delivery-manager holder when eligible under the normal chain and never through assignment, and HR in the HR role never approves, rejects, decides or records an adjustment; the assignment covers that block, extension request or adjustment-needed flag until it is decided and is re-checked at each new block version and at decision time; the history records who assigned, when, why and the eligibility result; an entry unresolved after the waiting-days number escalates to an escalation contact; the waiting days (3 working days) and the contact (the module's business owner) are provisional defaults P3 and P8, not approved policy; routing, reassignment and queue notices are event notifications, separate from the weekly reminder cap, and must not bypass any notification controls the product defines; the alternate decides only that block, the history records the reason and who decided it, and the shared decision rules in the constraints apply equally to blocks, extension requests and adjustments.
- **CAP-23**
  - **intent:** A week fully covered by approved leave in the existing HRMS is a leave-only sheet, so the employee has nothing to submit and no reviewer has anything to approve.
  - **success:** A sheet is leave-only when every working day from Monday to Friday of the timesheet week is covered by approved HRMS leave; Saturday and Sunday are ignored and public holidays are not recognised in v1, so a holiday counts as covered only if HRMS records approved leave for it; PTO is derived from HRMS at 8 hours per covered working day and never entered by the employee; the leave-only classification is shown clearly and is a sheet type, not a fifth state; there is no employee submission, no reviewer approval, no late mark and no reminder, and the sheet is excluded from the unpublished list; finance reports zero project hours for it; eligibility is recalculated when HRMS leave is approved, cancelled or amended, and if HRMS leave data cannot be verified the classification fails closed, the sheet keeps its current type and is re-evaluated at the next HRMS event or the sweep; a sheet converts to or from leave-only automatically only when it has no project blocks, no publication, no recorded decision (a block decision, an extension decision or an adjustment entry) and no late mark; if any of those exist the case is flagged for authorized, audited resolution and the sheet is never silently converted, republished, stripped of history or used to rewrite an issued export; a leave-only sheet is not published and sends no publication notice; when leave is cancelled and the week is no longer fully covered, a sheet that has none of those becomes a normal sheet to publish and its deadline counts from publication as usual; when a submission races a leave approval the submission wins and the sheet is flagged, not converted.
- **CAP-24**
  - **intent:** A correction to approved block hours is recorded as a dated adjustment entry on top of the original approved block.
  - **success:** The original approved block is preserved and never edited; an adjustment entry is dated and carries the affected block, the change in hours, the net hours before and after, a reason, who recorded it and the eligibility result, and is initiated and recorded directly by an actor who passes the Eligible Decider rule at the time of recording: the named reviewer when eligible or an eligible alternate in the current tier of the alternate chain (CAP-21), either of whom may act at any time under the shared decision rules, so the first valid recording wins and the other actor is told; a reason is mandatory and the named reviewer is notified on every adjustment; never by an employee on their own block, never by that sheet's Project 1 owner or delegate and never by HR; there is no separate adjustment-request object: when neither the named reviewer nor any alternate is eligible HR raises an adjustment-needed flag on the approved block with a reason, the flag (open, assigned or recorded) enters the HR exception queue (CAP-22), and HR assigns an eligible alternate who then records the adjustment; HR may raise and manage the flag (assign, track) but never records or approves an adjustment, and the queue records none; each adjustment is bound to the net hours the recorder reviewed, eligibility, block version and net hours are rechecked at recording time, and a stale or concurrent adjustment is refused with a message to reload; adjustments follow the 0.5-hour step; an adjustment that would make the block's net approved hours negative is refused; the net roll-up includes adjustments without counting the block twice; previously issued exports stay unchanged and later exports include the dated adjustment history; HR can view adjustment history for audit but cannot create, modify, approve or reject; the employee is notified when an adjustment is recorded, with the affected reporting week, the change in hours, the reason and the resulting net hours; whether the 40-hour cap applies to adjustments, a time limit for recording one, and an employee dispute route are deferred to V2 and not set.

## Constraints

- A sheet has at most 5 project blocks; each has project, tasks/activities and hours entered by the employee and a reviewer taken from the HRMS assignment and shown read-only.
- PTO is entered in hours, 8 per day, except on a leave-only sheet where it is derived from HRMS (CAP-23).
- Project hours plus PTO total at most 40 per week; a sheet above 40 is refused with the reason shown, and a refused over-40 attempt never changes the lateness decision (an HRMS-caused refusal follows CAP-10); the cap is flat, with no overtime or holiday handling in v1.
- The submitter's name and email come from the signed-in identity, never from form fields.
- The billable flag lives on the project.
- Identity, project list, the employee's primary project and each project's owner and reviewer come from the existing HRMS, not admin-maintained lists; approved-leave and active status are also read from the HRMS and never maintained here.
- Approved hours are immutable; a later correction creates a recorded adjustment (CAP-24), never a silent edit.
- A late mark is visible to the employee, the project reviewer, the reporting manager and HR; the Project 1 owner sees only whether a sheet was on time or late. Late marks and approved hours are visible to HR, and HR decides any consequences for employees; the module never withholds, delays or reduces pay itself, and it publishes no public individual rankings or shaming lists.
- A sheet is never marked late unless it was published; the deadline counts from publication.
- An approved sheet is never reported to HR as incorrect; the late mark is the only mark that can attach to an approved sheet; adjustment entries and change history are records, not marks, and HR sees adjustment history for audit only.
- An employee has exactly one sheet per week; a second sheet for the same employee and week is refused.
- The timesheet week runs Monday 00:00 to the next Monday 00:00 in Asia/Kolkata; timestamps are stored in UTC and shown in IST.
- A sheet is in exactly one state: draft, submitted, changes required or approved, derived from its project blocks, each of which is draft, submitted, rejected or approved; approved means locked and happens when every block is approved; the sheet shows approved when every block is approved, otherwise changes required while any block is rejected and not yet resubmitted, otherwise submitted, with each block's own status always shown; approved blocks stay locked when another block is rejected or reopened. Each named project reviewer decides only their own block, and no one decides a block on their own sheet (CAP-21). A leave-only sheet (CAP-23) is a sheet type, not a fifth state, and each block resubmission creates a new block version. Terms are defined in `glossary.md`.
- The Project 1 owner and any delegate of a sheet never approve, reject, decide or record an adjustment for a block or an extension request on that sheet and are never its alternate or HR-assigned decider; a named reviewer who is that sheet's Project 1 owner or delegate is ineligible for that sheet only and the block routes down the alternate chain; the restriction is scoped to that employee's sheet (owner decision 2026-10-11), and the existing statement that the Project 1 owner never approves is unchanged; publishing and deciding are separate permissions: an employee who is their own sheet's owner or delegate may publish it, which never lets them decide any part of it (owner decision D14).
- Eligible Decider rule (the single definition for deciding a block or an extension request, recording an adjustment and being assigned from the HR queue): at the instant of the decision, judged on authoritative HRMS data, a person is eligible only if they are not the employee whose sheet it is, not the named reviewer when that reviewer is conflicted, not that sheet's Project 1 owner (the owner snapshotted at publication and the current HRMS owner, and every candidate owner while a conflict or multiple owners are flagged) or delegate (one whose naming is in effect, or who published that sheet under an earlier naming), active in the existing HRMS and not on approved leave; for a queue assignment they must also hold no HR role. An HR-role holder who is also a reporting manager or delivery-manager holder may decide in that separate capacity through the normal chain only. Designating a delegate is independent of this rule and grants no eligibility by itself; whether a delegate may decide a given sheet is judged by this rule at decision time, where being that sheet's delegate, including by having published it, is an exclusion. The alternate chain is tiered (CAP-21): the reporting manager when eligible, then delivery-manager holders only when the reporting manager is ineligible.
- Owner and delegate status is judged at the decision instant: the owner from both the assignment snapshotted at publication and the current HRMS assignment, either one excluding, and the delegate from the module's naming records in effect then or the record that the person published that sheet; publication-time snapshots are preserved, an HRMS ownership or assignment change after publication flags the affected pending item for resolution and never rewrites a recorded decision; each delegate naming, change and expiry is audited and pending decisions are re-evaluated when one occurs.
- HRMS outage policy: when HRMS data an action needs is unavailable or cannot be verified, the action fails closed and never completes on guessed or stale data: an eligibility check, a submit, a publish and a copy are refused with a message to retry, and a leave-only classification leaves the sheet as it is until the next HRMS event or the sweep; each failed attempt records its timestamp, the actor and the affected action; lateness after an HRMS-caused refusal follows CAP-10 and is separate from an over-40 refusal; a retroactive HRMS change flags the affected records for audited resolution and never silently rewrites a recorded decision; the module records observed HRMS outage windows (CAP-10).
- HRMS facts each action verifies (provisional, owner decision D22): submit: employee active, the primary project snapshotted at publication and each project's reviewer; publish: owner, delegate naming and publisher active; copy: each project's reviewers; decide, record or assign: the Eligible Decider facts (active, approved leave, owner, delegate, HR role); leave classification: approved leave. Freshness is provisional default P14: a verified HRMS read may be at most 5 minutes old, until the architecture step defines cache invalidation, which eligibility checks are critical and failure behavior; stale data never authorizes an action when current eligibility cannot be established, and the action fails closed.
- Shared decision rules for blocks, extension requests and adjustments: eligibility is checked when the item is routed or created, at each new version, at decision time, on HRMS change events and by the periodic sweep; the normal decider, when eligible, and the eligible alternate of the current tier (CAP-21) may both act, only one valid decision is recorded and the other actor is told, and an item is never automatically routed back; a pending extension request reroutes as a block does; each decision binds to the version seen (the block version for a block, the request as stored for an extension request, the net hours reviewed for an adjustment) and a stale action is refused with a message to reload; every decision records who, when and the eligibility result; when nobody is eligible the item goes to the HR exception queue.
- Hours are entered per project per week, never per day; every employee, including exempt IT staff, submits weekly effort only.
- Submitted sheets, approved sheets and their change history are kept at least 3 years, a generic rule modelled on the US; the owner chose it with no country-specific follow-up.
- A late, rejected or incorrect sheet never changes pay for hours worked; this module reports hours and never withholds, delays or reduces pay, and the late penalty is only a recorded late mark.
- Reminders are scheduled against UTC and sent at most once per person, reminder kind and week, so a retry or a clock change never sends a duplicate or a late reminder; routing, reassignment and exception-queue notices are event notifications, not reminders, so the weekly cap does not apply to them, and they must not bypass any notification controls the product defines.

## Non-goals

- Payroll calculation or processing, leave balances or leave approval.
- Any payroll handoff: loss-of-pay (LOP) days, payable days, the payroll cut-off, and the payroll data format are out of scope (CAP-15 retired).
- Invoicing; this initiative supplies billable hours, not invoices.
- Attendance or clock-in tracking.
- Auto-drafting the week from calendar or tickets in v1 (see `premium-tier.md`).
- Ambient time capture, an hours marketplace, and a continuous rolling week.
- Choosing the tech stack, hosting, observability, backups or CI/CD; these belong to the architecture step, not this spec.

## Success signal

An employee completes and submits a full weekly sheet in one sitting, and billing can read that week's approved hours per project without re-keying anything.

## Assumptions

- Only Project 1 is required; Projects 2-5 are optional, although the source form marks every field required.
- Hours are in 0.5 steps.
- v1 serves about 160 employees and 20 projects (owner); sizing detail belongs to the architecture step.
- The 15-hour jump in CAP-11 is a starting value for the owner to set.
- Reopening is only by the original employee, only for rejected or undecided blocks, and never after a block is approved.
- Approvals (CAP-6) stay in the core product although competitors gate them behind paid plans, because they are core in an HRMS module.
- All capabilities ship free for now with no pricing; `premium-tier.md` lists future candidates only, and its competitor prices come from vendor pages without a second source.
- The owner holds that reminders and copy-last-week raise on-time submission; this is a belief, not research evidence, and the on-time rate is still to be measured, with no target set.
- The submission deadline is the publication timestamp plus 24 hours (owner, 2026-10-11, replacing the earlier 11:59 pm UTC rule); a late sheet is still accepted, marked late, and carries the late penalty.
- Publication by the Project 1 owner opens the whole sheet for the employee, including Projects 2-5 (owner confirmed).
- The deadline is a fixed instant for every employee regardless of local time zone.
- If a sheet is published on a day other than Friday, the deadline is still 24 hours after that publication.
- Late-sheet handling never delays pay or hard-locks submission; this rests on US guidance and has not had legal review. The owner accepts this and the 3-year retention with no legal review.
- Approved means locked, with no separate billed state, because invoicing is a non-goal (confirmed).
- Utilization and realization use total available hours as the denominator in `glossary.md` (confirmed).
- The named project reviewer approves project hours; line-manager and finance approval roles are out of scope (confirmed; the reporting manager has a read-only view, CAP-19, and decides only as the first eligible decider in the alternate chain, CAP-21 and CAP-22).
- The late penalty is the late mark: a recorded mark counted per employee (confirmed by the owner; visibility in the constraints); HR decides any consequences (decision A).
- An approved extension request records a waiver of the late penalty for that sheet and the original late mark stays in the audit history; the HRMS-assigned reviewer of the employee's Project 1 decides it when eligible, otherwise the eligible alternate chain, and HR never decides in the HR role; there is no limit on how many an employee requests.
- An adjustment entry on approved block hours is recorded with a reason and who made it, by the named reviewer when eligible or an eligible alternate from the alternate chain (which includes the reporting manager) who passes the Eligible Decider rule at recording time, the first valid recording winning (owner decision D17); conflict is judged per sheet at the decision instant.
- HR has read-only access to sheets, hours, late marks and adjustment history; its only write actions are publishing a sheet when neither the Project 1 owner nor their delegate has, assigning an alternate decider from the exception queue (CAP-22), and raising an adjustment-needed flag with a reason (CAP-24); the assignee must not be the employee, the named reviewer, that sheet's Project 1 owner or delegate, an inactive employee, one on approved leave, or any HR-role holder (owner confirmed).
- A delegate publisher is an active employee not on approved leave when named and explicitly designated by the Project 1 owner, with designation independent of the Eligible Decider rule (owner decision D14); a naming is owner plus the current timesheet week only and expires at the Monday 00:00 IST that ends it (D25); the delegate may publish and see submission status (CAP-20) but never approves or rejects, and stays excluded from deciding a sheet they published after the naming expires (D26) (owner confirmed).
- The HR publish cut-off is Friday 12:00 PM IST of the timesheet week, held as the fixed instant Friday 06:30 UTC, for all projects (owner confirmed).
- The delivery-manager role is designated in the existing HRMS by an authorized HR administrator; several holders are allowed, any eligible holder may decide and the first valid decision wins (owner confirmed 2026-10-11); which holders count for a given employee is provisional default P11.
- The HRMS identifies one primary project per employee and each project's owner and reviewer; a missing or conflicting assignment is flagged for resolution and never guessed (owner confirmed 2026-10-11), and while it is unresolved the assignment-pending path in CAP-2 applies with its four reason codes (owner decisions D16, D24); the primary project and owner are snapshotted at publication (D23).
- The alternate approver decides only the block routed to them; the rest of the sheet follows its normal reviewers, and the sheet locks only when every block is approved.
- A sheet may be published for the current week or a past week; no maximum publication window is set, so none applies until the owner sets one (owner confirmed).
- A block whose named reviewer is inactive in HRMS or on approved leave goes to the alternate approver chain; the days an undecided block waits before escalating from the queue are provisional default P3 (owner confirmed the routing).
- Leave-only coverage counts working days Monday to Friday in Asia/Kolkata, ignores Saturday and Sunday, and does not recognise public holidays in v1, so a holiday counts as covered only when HRMS records approved leave for it; PTO on a leave-only sheet is 8 hours per covered working day, 40 for a full week (owner approved the defaults 2026-10-11).
- A recorded decision means a block approve or reject, an extension decision or an adjustment entry; the HR notice on entry to the exception queue is an event notification like routing and reassignment; a named reviewer may decide only while eligible, so approved leave blocks deciding (owner confirmed, 2026-10-11).
- Details inferred from owner decisions D21, D23 and D24, for the owner to confirm: an observed outage window holds a late mark when it overlaps the period between publication and the deadline; when an assignment-pending flag leaves a published sheet, a new snapshot is added and the earlier one is kept; a sheet with missing or multiple owners is published by HR after the cut-off, and no owner guessed publishes it before.
- The tiered alternate chain, adjustment authority, extension routing class, outage-attempt qualification, assignment snapshot, four reason codes, week-bound delegate naming and persistent delegate exclusion are owner decisions D18 to D26 (2026-10-11); the 5-minute freshness window (P14) is provisional.
- An export issued to finance is a dated snapshot that is retained and never rewritten; corrections appear as dated adjustment entries in a later export.

## Provisional Defaults

These are not approved requirements. Each is a safe default for build, kept separate from the decisions above and listed in Open Questions until the confirmation shown is given.

- **P1** Delegate: any active employee not on approved leave when named who is explicitly designated by the owner; a narrower HRMS role is pending (HR or business).
- **P2** No maximum publication window (business, only if a limit is wanted).
- **P3** HR exception queue escalation threshold: 3 working days, configurable (owner accepted as a provisional default 2026-10-11; HR or business confirmation still required).
- **P4** Partial-week leave: the employee reminder is suppressed only on a day the employee is on approved leave at the send instant (HR).
- **P5** A refused over-40 attempt is not a submission; the first successful submission time counts (business).
- **P6** A reviewer sees their own block plus the sheet's total hours only, with no other block content, accomplishments or learnings (UX and business).
- **P7** Periodic sweep once a day; employee reminder 6 hours before the deadline; reviewer reminder Monday 09:00 IST once a week (owner accepted as a provisional default 2026-10-11; HR or business confirmation still required).
- **P8** Escalation contact: the module's business owner named in configuration (owner accepted as a provisional default 2026-10-11; business confirms).
- **P9** An extension may be requested any time before the sheet is approved and not after; the SM-1 treatment of waived marks stays an owner decision (business).
- **P10** Notification policy: no user opt-out in V1; event notices batched per recipient per hour (owner accepted as a provisional default 2026-10-11; product or HR confirmation still required).
- **P11** Delivery-manager scope: all holders of the role (business).
- **P12** The Project 1 block is the employee's HRMS primary project as snapshotted at publication, and owner designation uses it regardless of the hours logged, except while the assignment-pending path applies (business).
- **P13** An HR-role holder who is also a delivery-manager holder may decide in that capacity through the chain and never through queue assignment, as for the reporting manager (owner).
- **P14** HRMS verified-read freshness: a verified read may be at most 5 minutes old; provisional until the architecture step defines cache invalidation, which eligibility checks are critical and failure behavior; stale data never authorizes an action when current eligibility cannot be established (owner accepted provisionally 2026-10-11, D22).
- **P15** Source-data corrections for flagged HRMS assignments: PROPOSED only, not approved: the HRMS admin team corrects them with a one-working-day target; this is not an SLA until the business approves it.
